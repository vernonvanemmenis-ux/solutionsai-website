## TL;DR — The Thesis

The value of "doing AI development" is trending toward zero. Cheap, easily-deployable AI is collapsing org charts (200-person services firm → 15-person firm + workflows). The winning AI-services playbook is **not** a lifestyle agency selling automations — it's a **full-stack AI consulting firm with a named methodology, value-based pricing, and a path through the $3M → $10M EBITDA "rerate line"** that re-prices service businesses from 1–2× earnings to 8–15×+ multiples. That jump is the $100M exit thesis.

Mid-market companies ($10M–$250M revenue, already systematized with SOPs/KPIs) are the prime customers. They have processes you can encode; enterprises "throw bodies at problems" and SMBs lack the operational substrate.

Buyers are **buying relief from pressure**, not technology. Frame everything around that.

## The Big Frames

### 1. "Yesterday's services firm" vs "Tomorrow's services firm"
- **Yesterday:** CEO → many directors → 200+ headcount doing linear labor.
- **Tomorrow:** CEO → 2 directors → ~15 people + ~10 workflows producing compounding leverage.

### 2. Two diverging arrows (the 2027 fork)
- **Down (dies):** thin wrappers, weekend automations, undifferentiated implementations.
- **Up (survives):** production systems with measurable ROI, embedded into operations, owned by the client.
> "Most AI work being sold today won't survive 2027." (~26:48)

### 3. AI Agency Playbooks (2×2: Technical Depth × Contract Size)
The "Target Zone" is high-depth + high-contract — only two business models live there:
- **Full-stack consulting firm**
- **PE-backed roll-up**

### 4. The $3M EBITDA Rerate Line ("the Hermozi jump")
- Under ~$5M revenue: businesses sell at **1–2× earnings**.
- Above the line: multiples rerate to **8–15×+ EBITDA** ("AI-native premium").
- $3M → $10M EBITDA = ~13× enterprise-value growth.

### 5. The Worst Flywheel Possible (the buyer's mental state)
Three pressures converge on one exhausted executive:
1. **Board & investors** demanding an AI strategy
2. **Industry peers** hyping inflated AI wins
3. **Internal teams** with conflicting experiments and views

Result: the buyer isn't short on answers — they're drowning in them. Your job is **clear coherent answer + relief**, not more technology.

## The Four-Engagement Funnel (CustomAI Studio's actual offer ladder)

> "Each stage earns the next."

| # | Engagement | Duration | Price | What it produces |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | **AI Workshop** | 2 sessions (1hr + 2–3hr) | ~$1K–$2.5K (fixed) | Two-session diagnostic; alignment on what AI is/isn't; trust |
| 02 | **AI Blueprint** | 2–6 weeks | $15K–$35K (his now); ceiling $50K–$250K | ACS + workflow diagnostic; business case for the board; deliverable portable to any dev shop |
| 03 | **Custom AI Project** | Scoped | Scoped to engagement | Build, ship, support adoption, teach the client team |
| 04 | **AI Technology Partnership** | 10+ months, ongoing | **$100K–$2M+ MRR + 15–25% revenue share** + T&M retainer | Embedded Solution Architect + Technical Project Manager |

Why the Partnership works: it's the **growth-marketer model applied to AI delivery** — you get a budget, take % of top-line, incentives align. Reframe vs T&M: "I'm handing you ~10 employees that work 24/7, are super smart, and cost a fraction" — that can't be priced by the hour.

**Where ROI is easiest to sell:** sales & marketing (emails sent → revenue is a direct line). Internal ops/accounting harder to attribute.

## The AgenticOS Framework (their named methodology)

> "Event-driven. Deterministic where possible. Humans where they matter."

Components:
- **Webhook** (input)
- **Event Classifier** — deterministic rules with model fallback
- **Workflow Engine** — state machine + DWF runtime
- **Task Executor (core):**
  - *Deterministic:* API calls, SQL queries (execute directly)
  - *Generation:* focused model call
  - *Approval:* queue for review (blocks until approved)
- **Hook System** — post-action, pipeline, audit, sync, deadlines ("every hook is also a learning event")
- **Master DB** (sync)
- **Feedback loop** — performance metrics, audit logs, learning consolidation → updates Classifier, Workflow Engine, Task Executor

**Positioning point:** "The framework is the product. The hours are the implementation." Without a named methodology you're a vendor; with one you're a firm with a point of view (cf. McKinsey Three Horizons, BCG Strategy Palette, Bain RAPID).

**Niching:** *not* by industry — by **process**.

## 5 Things Devin Wishes He'd Known Sooner

1. **Pick a tier and commit** (lifestyle vs product vs full-stack consulting — pick one).
2. **Package the service inside a framework** (not "tell me what you want and I'll build it").
3. **Charge what the value is worth** (T&M / hourly → zero; performance pricing requires you to actually deliver).
4. **Build the funnel before you need it** (workshops → blueprints → projects → partnerships, in chronological skill order).
5. **Hire for the company you want to be** (after you've done the full process yourself end-to-end and built the SOP).

## Implications for the SolutionsAI Website Redesign

Direct translation of the talk into site copy/IA decisions:

1. **Reposition the hero from "agency" → "AI consulting / AI-native transformation."** Lead with "relief from AI pressure," not with capabilities. Audience = stressed executives at mid-market companies ($10M–$250M).
2. **Surface a named methodology.** Don't say "we build custom AI" — give the process a name and show its diagram (à la AgenticOS). This is the single biggest credibility lever in the talk.
3. **Show a tiered engagement ladder** on the site that mirrors the four-engagement funnel: Workshop → Blueprint → Project → Partnership. Even if pricing isn't shown, the *shape* of the offer should be visible.
4. **Add AI Training & Enablement** as a real service card (already partly done — see commit `2932d1c`). That maps to the "Workshop" rung of the ladder and the "growing services" tier.
5. **Lead with ROI/proof, not technology.** Refund-rate-style numbers, ticket-volume numbers, headcount-equivalents. Avoid "we use Claude / RAG / agents" framing.
6. **Drop "agency" language** wherever it implies labor-for-hire. Prefer "AI consulting firm," "AI partner," or "AI-native transformation."
7. **Target audience explicitly:** mid-market operators with existing SOPs/KPIs. The site should *say* this, so SMBs and pure-enterprise leads self-deselect.
8. **Avoid the 8 traps** in the *copy*: don't pitch hours, don't sell features, don't position generically.
